## Abstract.
This  study  explored  the  development  and  evaluation  of  a  machine  learning  algorithm  designed  to  create
personalized  learning  pathways  for  students  in  higher  education,  based  on  their  strengths,  weaknesses,  and
career goals. Recognizing the limitations of traditional educational approaches, the research utilized a simulated
dataset  that  included  variables  such  as  GPA,  attendance  rates,  engagement  scores,  and  career  aspirations.  A
Random Forest  model  was  employed  to  predict  student  retention  outcomes,  effectively  identifying  at-risk
individuals  and  providing  tailored  recommendations  for  academic  success.  The  findings  demonstrated  that
personalized learning pathways significantly enhanced student engagement and retention rates, offering insights
into   the   effectiveness   of   data-driven   strategies.   Additionally,   the   study   highlighted   the   importance   of
implementing  comprehensive  data  analytics  frameworks  and  enhancing  faculty  training  to  support these
initiatives.  Overall,  the  research  underscored  the  transformative  potential  of  machine  learning  in  fostering  a
more  supportive  and  individualized  educational  experience,  ultimately  preparing  students  more  effectively  for
their future careers.
Key Words: Machine Learning, Personalized Learning Pathways and University Students
Background of the Study
Personalized  learning  pathways  represent  a  transformative  approach  in  education,  aiming  to  tailor  learning
experiences  to  meet  the  unique  needs  and  aspirations  of  individual  students (Pauline,  2023).  As  educational
institutions  increasingly  recognize  that  students  come  with  varying  backgrounds,  strengths,  weaknesses,  and
career goals, the traditional one-size-fits-all model of teaching is becoming less effective (Lonah Chongeywo &
Alex,  2023).  This  shift  in  focus  is  particularly  important  in  higher  education,  where  students  are  not  only
seeking  knowledge  but  also  preparing  for  specific  careers  that  require  specialized  skills  and  competencies
(Kazaara, 2023). The  concept  of  personalized  learning  is  grounded  in  the  belief  that  students  learn  best  when
they  can  engage  with  content  that  is  relevant  to  their  interests  and  aligned  with  their  personal  goals.  Research
has shown that personalized learning can lead to improved academic outcomes, higher engagement levels, and
increased  motivation  among  students (Abbas  et  al.,  2020).  However,  implementing  personalized  learning  at
scale  poses  significant  challenges,  particularly  in  managing  the  vast  amounts  of  data  generated  by  students'
performance and learning behaviors (Ozturk, 2019).
Machine learning (ML) offers promising solutions to these challenges by providing powerful tools for analysing
complex  datasets  and  identifying  patterns  that  can  inform  personalized  learning  strategies (Komakech,  2015).
By  leveraging  ML  algorithms,  educational  institutions  can  develop  systems  that analyse individual  student
data—such  as  academic  performance,  learning  styles,  and  engagement  metrics—to  create  tailored  learning
pathways (Khassawneh  et  al.,  2018).  These  pathways  can  include  recommendations  for  specific  courses,
resources, and learning activities that align with each student's strengths and weaknesses. Moreover, as students

## Problem Statement
In  the  contemporary educational  landscape,  one  of  the  foremost  challenges  faced  by  higher  education
institutions  is  the  effective  personalization  of  learning  experiences  to  cater  to  the  diverse  needs  of  students.
Traditional  educational  models  often  fail  to  account  for  individual  differences  in  learning  styles,  strengths,
weaknesses,  and  career  aspirations,  leading  to  disengagement  and  suboptimal  academic  performance (Peter  et
al.,  2023). As  a  result,  many  students  struggle  to  navigate  their  educational  pathways,  which  can  hinder  their
academic   success   and   diminish   their   preparedness   for   future   careers.   Despite   the   growing   interest   in
personalized  learning  approaches,  institutions  often  lack  the  tools  and  frameworks  needed  to  implement  these
strategies  effectively.  Current  methods  for  advising  and  course  selection  are  frequently  reactive,  relying  on
historical  performance data  without incorporating real-time  insights or aligning  with students' long-term career
goals (Topuz  et  al.,  2023).  This  gap  presents  a  significant  barrier  to  student  engagement  and  retention,  as
students may feel unsupported in reaching their full potential.
Additionally,  the  vast  amounts  of  data  generated  by  students—including  grades,  attendance,  participation  in
extracurricular   activities,   and   self-reported   interests—remain   underutilized.   Without   advanced   analytical
methods, institutions struggle to extract actionable insights from this data, making it difficult to provide tailored
recommendations   that   enhance   the   learning   experience (Zhao   et   al.,   2023).   Furthermore,   the   lack   of
personalized  guidance  can  lead  to  students  making  uninformed  choices  about  their  courses  and  career  paths,
resulting  in  increased  dropout  rates  and  dissatisfaction  with  their  educational  experience.  This  study  aims  to
address these challenges by developing a simple machine learning algorithm that suggests personalized learning

## Methodology
Developing the Machine Learning Algorithm for Personalized Learning Pathways
To  achieve  the  first  objective  of  developing  a  machine  learning  algorithm  for  personalized  learning  pathways,
the research will begin with the collection of a comprehensive dataset that includes various student-related data
points.  This  dataset  will  encompass  academic  performance  metrics  (e.g.,  grades,  GPAs),  attendance  records,
engagement   scores   from   activities   and   assessments,   and   self-reported career   goals.   Data   preprocessing
techniques  will  be  employed  to  clean  the  dataset,  addressing  missing  values  and  normalizing  data  to  ensure
uniformity (Chen & Ding, 2023). Feature selection methods were applied to identify the most relevant attributes
that contribute to student success and satisfaction. Subsequently, a supervised learning approach will be utilized,
with   algorithms   such   as  decision   trees,   random   forests,   or   support   vector   machines  selected   for   their
effectiveness  in  classification  tasks. The  model  will  be  trained  using  a  subset  of  the  dataset,  with  a  portion
reserved for validation to optimize its parameters (Onyema et al., 2022).
Testing the Effectiveness of the Algorithm
For  the  second  objective,  the  effectiveness  of  the  personalized  learning  pathways  algorithm was rigorously
evaluated through a series of tests and metrics. After training the model, the validation dataset used to assess its
predictive accuracy, utilizing measures such as precision, recall, and F1-score to quantify performance (Bhushan
et al., 2023). Additionally, A/B testing will be implemented in a real-world educational setting, where a control
group  of  students  followed traditional  advising  methods  while  an  experimental  group received personalized
learning  pathway  recommendations  generated  by  the  algorithm.  This  setup  enabled a  comparative  analysis  of
outcomes,  focusing  on  metrics  like  student  engagement,  course  completion  rates,  and  overall  satisfaction
through  surveys  and  academic  performance  tracking.  Statistical  analysis were conducted  to  determine  the
significance  of  the  results,  providing  insights  into  the  algorithm's  impact  on  student  success (Nelson  et  al.,
## 2022).
Reusing the Model on Other Datasets
To ensure the algorithm's versatility and applicability across different contexts, the final step of the methodology
involved developing  a  framework  for  reusing  the  model  on  other  datasets.  This  included creating  a  modular
architecture  that  allows  for  easy  integration  of  new  student data  from  various  institutions  or  educational
